The Battle of Mytros

A War of Bronze, Will & Cunning — Player's Field Guide

A mass combat system for Odyssey of the Dragonlords — where every stat matters, every commander counts, and your choices shape the tide of battle.

I

Legion Stats

Every legion is defined by three stats: Vitality, Morale, and Wit. Each stat is a single total value assigned by the DM, reflecting the combined quality of the legion's troops, training, equipment, and the influence of their commander.

Stat Totals Recommended total across all three stats: 10–18 points. Stronger legions led by legendary commanders sit at the higher end; weaker or green legions at the lower end.
⚔ Vitality
Stat of Survival

Physical toughness, endurance, and discipline under fire. Invest in Vitality for legions you intend to grind the enemy with attrition — or that must hold a critical position.

Drives The Clash (Phase 3 of battle)
Governs the Recovery Check (injury intake)
🔥 Morale
Stat of Momentum

Fighting spirit, cohesion, and the willingness to press the attack. Invest in Morale for aggressive legions that need to win fast and inspire those around them.

Drives The Charge (Phase 2 of battle)
Governs the Hope Check (morale gain/loss)
Acts as Protection in Commander Casualty checks
Caps at 10; dropping to 0 causes a rout
🧠 Wit
Stat of Control

Tactical discipline, adaptability, and cunning. Invest in Wit for legions that need to punch above their weight through superior positioning.

Drives Reconnaissance (allied intelligence)
Drives The Maneuver (Phase 1 of battle)
Governs the Salvage Check (battlefield gains)
II

Commander Tags

Each commander has 2–3 tags that define their unique abilities. Every tag is a permanent, always-on passive bonus — there are no daily charges, no activation costs, and no refresh cycles. Tags are always on.

Conditional Tags If a tag's condition cannot be met (e.g., Zealot requires Morale 7+, but the legion is at 6), the tag simply has no effect until the condition is satisfied again. A newly assigned replacement commander arrives with their own tags, which take effect immediately.

III

Round Structure

Each round represents roughly one hour of battle. The round flows through five phases in order. The battle continues indefinitely until a termination condition is met.

End of Battle Conditions The battle ends when: (1) one side has no legions remaining, (2) all major story events have been resolved and the DM declares the battle concluded, or (3) a battle-ending major event occurs (e.g. Kentimane's defeat).
IV

Battle Resolution

When opposing legions meet in the same section, battle occurs in three sequential phases. Each phase, both sides roll 1d20 + stat. Subtract the enemy's total from yours — the result (positive or negative) is added to a single running Battle Score. After all three phases, a positive score means you win; negative means the enemy wins; zero is a tie.

Phase 1: The Maneuver (Wit)

Both sides roll 1d20 + Wit. The differential (your roll minus enemy roll) is added to the Battle Score. If your differential is positive, you win the phase and choose one benefit. If tied (0), no benefit is granted.

Flanking Position
+1d4 to your Charge roll (Phase 2)
Defensive Footing
+1d2 to your Clash roll (Phase 3)
Disrupted Formation
Enemy suffers −1 to both Charge and Clash rolls
Seized Initiative
If you win the overall battle, enemy takes +1d2 extra injury in aftermath

Phase 2: The Charge (Morale)

Both sides roll 1d20 + Morale. The differential is added to the Battle Score. The Charge winner (positive differential) gains +1 to their Clash roll. If tied, no cascade bonus.

Phase 3: The Clash (Vitality)

Both sides roll 1d20 + Vitality. The differential is added to the Battle Score.

Determining the Victor

The Battle Score is the sum of all three phase differentials. Positive means your side wins; negative means the enemy wins. The magnitude determines the severity of aftermath consequences (see Commander Casualty Check).

Tie-Breaker If the Battle Score is exactly 0, a sudden-death contested Vitality roll breaks the tie. If that also ties, reroll until one side wins.

Fortification

A section can be fortified after being held for a full round. Fortification grants +1 to all rolls for the defending side and is lost when the opposing side claims the section.

💀 Commander Casualty

1d100 vs Final Death Target (min 1%)
Situation Base %
Won the battle 6%
Lost the battle 12%
Lost by 15+ on Battle Score 20%

Protection = legion's current Morale. Subtract Protection from Base % to get Final Death Target (minimum 1%). Roll 1d100 — at or under the target, the commander falls.

Commander Casualty Examples

Example 1: A legion with 5 Morale loses a battle. Base Death Chance 12% − 5 Protection = 7% Final Death Target. On a 1–7, the commander dies.
Example 2: A legion with 2 Morale loses by 15+ on the Battle Score. Base Death Chance 20% − 2 Protection = 18% Final Death Target. Extremely dangerous.
Example 3: A legion with 8 Morale wins the battle. Base Death Chance 6% − 8 Protection = capped to 1% Final Death Target. Only a 1 kills the commander.
On Commander Death The legion immediately loses 1 Morale. A replacement commander takes command at the start of the next round with their own tags. If no reserves remain, the legion operates without a commander — no tags apply.
Rout Rules When Morale hits 0: legion retreats up to 3 sections toward nearest allied territory and cannot act next round (unless it has the Relentless tag). If an enemy occupies a routed legion's section while it has 0 Morale and no allied legion is present, the routed legion is disbanded. Phase 5 passive recovery (+1 Morale) is enough to rally a routed legion.
VI

Player Characters as Fast Response

The PCs are the allied side's only fast response capability. After battle pairings are revealed in Phase 3, each PC chooses a battle to influence. Multiple PCs can stack in the same battle — a powerful choice with a real cost.

Rally

Deployment Limit No more than 2 PCs may deploy to the same battle in a single round. Remaining PCs must deploy elsewhere, Rally, or rest.

Resting

A PC may skip a round of deployment to take a short rest. This is important because Major Events (full D&D encounters) can strike at any time, and arriving with spent resources is costly.

This is not just a military contest — every round of fighting costs the city its people. Track a running Death Toll alongside the battle map. At the end of each Phase 5, add that round's deaths to the cumulative total.

X

Major Events

At dramatic moments, the DM triggers a major event that pulls some or all PCs into a full D&D encounter — a direct confrontation, a ritual to interrupt, a titan's challenge. The mass combat system handles everything between those moments.

If the players resolve a major event in the allied side's favor, they earn a permanent bonus that shifts the balance of the wider battle. The DM will reveal what's at stake when the moment arrives.

While PCs are away Legions continue fighting without PC support during a major event. Consider where your legions are and which battles they can hold on their own before the encounter begins.
