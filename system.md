# **THE BATTLE OF MYTROS**

## _A War of Bronze, Will, and Cunning_

A Mass Combat System for Odyssey of the Dragonlords

_Revamped rules for cinematic legion warfare where every stat matters, every commander counts, and your players shape the tide of battle._

#

# **Design Philosophy**

This system turns the Battle of Mytros into a strategic war resolved over a single continuous engagement. There are no battle days or artificial pauses. Legions clash across the city, commanders rise and fall (sometimes permanently), and the player characters act as elite rapid-deployment assets that tip the balance at critical moments. The battle continues until one side has no legions remaining, or until all major story events have been resolved and the DM declares the battle concluded.

Major story events pull the players into full D\&D encounters at the DM’s discretion, while the mass combat system handles everything between those dramatic beats.

Three stats define every legion: Vitality (staying power), Morale (fighting spirit), and Wit (tactical cunning). Each stat drives one battle phase, one aftermath check, and has additional uses that reward strategic investment. No stat is dead weight.

Commanders do not have personal combat stats. Instead, their influence is represented entirely through Commander Tags: powerful passive bonuses that shape how their legion fights. A commander’s identity is defined by their tags, not a stat block. This keeps bookkeeping lean while preserving meaningful strategic variety.

Commanders can die permanently in battle. The threat of losing a beloved ally or the satisfaction of seeing a hated enemy fall raises the emotional stakes far beyond dice rolls.

# **The Battlemap**

Divide Mytros into map sections. The recommended count is 2 times the number of allied legions, rounded up. Label each section for easy reference.

A section can be fortified after being held for a full round. Fortification grants \+1 to all rolls for the defending side and is lost when the opposing side claims the section.

#

# **Legions**

Each side fields a number of legions. Sydon’s forces should start with one more legion than the allied side. Reinforcements can arrive mid-battle to shift the balance.

## **Legion Stats**

Every legion has three stats: Vitality, Morale, and Wit. Each stat is a single total value assigned by the DM, reflecting the combined quality of the legion’s troops, training, equipment, and the influence of their commander.

Recommended stat total per legion: **10–18 points** distributed across the three stats. Stronger legions led by legendary commanders sit at the higher end; weaker or green legions at the lower end.

### **Vitality**

Physical toughness, endurance, and discipline under fire. Vitality is the stat of survival: it determines whether a legion can hold the line under punishment and whether it takes crippling losses. Invest in Vitality for legions you intend to grind down the enemy through attrition or that must hold a critical position.

### **Morale**

Fighting spirit, cohesion, and the willingness to press the attack. Morale is the stat of momentum: it governs the devastating first strike of a charge, whether troops keep their nerve after a loss, and whether a winning streak compounds into an unstoppable tide. Invest in Morale for aggressive legions that need to win fast and inspire others around them.

### **Wit**

Tactical discipline, adaptability, and cunning. Wit is the stat of control: it determines who dictates the terms of the battle, whether a legion can extract advantage from every encounter, and how well the allied side reads the enemy’s plans. Invest in Wit for legions that need to punch above their weight through superior positioning.

# **Commander Tags**

Each commander has 2–3 tags that define their unique abilities. Every tag is a passive bonus that applies automatically whenever the relevant condition is met. There are no daily charges, no activation costs, and no refresh cycles. Tags are always on.

If a tag’s condition cannot be met (e.g., Zealot requires Morale 7+, but the legion is at 5), the tag simply has no effect until the condition is satisfied again.

A newly assigned replacement commander arrives with their own tags, which take effect immediately.

**Tag design principle:** tags tell a story about who the commander is. A cunning manipulator gets Tactician and Terrorizer. A beloved priest-warrior gets Rallier and Medic. A mounted warlord gets Vanguard and Brutal. A giant creature gets Bulwark and Warden. When assigning tags, ask: what would this commander’s troops say about them?

# **Round Structure**

Each round represents roughly one hour. The round flows through five phases. The battle continues indefinitely until a termination condition is met (see End of Battle).

## **Phase 1: Reconnaissance (Wit)**

One roll determines allied intelligence for the round.

**Roll:** 1d20 \+ highest Wit among all allied legions.

| Result      | Intelligence Gained                                                                |
| :---------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 10 or lower | No enemy movements revealed.                                                       |
| 11–14       | Learn the destination of 2 enemy legions.                                          |
| 15–18       | Learn the destinations of up to half the enemy legions (rounded up).               |
| 19–22       | Learn all enemy legion destinations.                                               |
| 23+         | Learn all enemy destinations and gain \+1 to all allied Maneuver rolls this round. |

## **Phase 2: Planning & Commitment**

The DM secretly records enemy movements. The DM reveals enemy movements based on the results of the previous phase. Players then plan and lock in all allied movements.

## **Phase 3: Reveal & Fast Response**

Enemy movements are revealed and battle pairings are determined. The enemy deploys fast response forces first, then the players deploy their PCs.

## **Phase 4: Battle**

All battles resolve simultaneously via dice rolls. If the DM triggers a major event, the relevant players are pulled into a full D\&D encounter instead.

## **Phase 5: Aftermath & Objectives**

Recovery, Hope, Salvage, and Commander Casualty checks for all legions that fought. Losing legions are pushed back 1 section (loser chooses direction). Legion sheets are updated.

Legions that did not fight this round recover 1 injury and regain \+1 Morale during this phase, representing time to regroup and tend wounds.

Check if any strategic objectives have been held for the required duration. Apply consequences for any destroyed objectives. Calculate civilian death toll. Check for end-of-battle conditions.

## **End of Battle**

**The battle ends when any of the following conditions are met:**

-   One side has no legions remaining (all destroyed, disbanded, or routed off the map).

-   All major story events have been resolved and the DM declares the battle concluded.

# **Player Characters as Fast Response**

The player characters are the allied side’s only fast response capability. Each PC acts as an individual rapid-deployment force. After battle pairings are revealed, each player chooses a battle to influence.

## **PC Deployment Options**

| Option             | Effect                                                                                                                      |
| :----------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reinforce          | \+1d4 to all battle and aftermath rolls for the allied legion.                                                              |
| Shock Assault      | \+1d6 to all three battle phase rolls only (no aftermath bonus).                                                            |
| Targeted Strike    | \+1d8 to one specific battle phase roll, plus advantage on that roll.                                                       |
| Shield the Wounded | \+1d8 to the first three aftermath rolls (Recovery, Hope, Salvage).                                                         |
| Protect            | The PC stays at the commander’s side throughout the battle. No Commander Casualty check is made for this legion this round. |

Multiple PCs can deploy to the same battle and their effects stack. Three PCs each choosing Reinforce gives \+3d4 to all rolls, but leaves every other battle unsupported. This is always a meaningful trade-off because the heroes cannot be everywhere at once.

Enemy fast response forces (storm elementals, dragonriders, etc.) exist as needed at the DM’s discretion and use the same table. Their deployment should feel like a reactive threat for the players to manage.

## **Resting**

A player may skip a round of deployment to take a short rest. This is important because major events (DM-triggered full D\&D encounters) can pull the party into combat at any time, and showing up with spent resources is costly.

## **Major Events**

At dramatic moments, the DM triggers a major event that pulls some or all PCs into a real D\&D combat encounter. These are entirely at the DM’s discretion: direct confrontation with an enemy commander, a dragon attack, a ritual to interrupt, or a titan’s challenge. The mass combat system handles everything between those events.

# **Battle Resolution**

When opposing legions meet in the same section, battle occurs. Each battle has three sequential phases. Winning earlier phases feeds advantages into later ones, rewarding legions that dominate from the start.

## **Phase 1: The Maneuver (Wit)**

Both sides roll 1d20 \+ Wit. The winner chooses one benefit for the rest of this battle:

| Maneuver Benefit    | Effect                                                                              |
| :------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Flanking Position   | \+1d4 to your Charge roll (Phase 2).                                                |
| Defensive Footing   | \+1d2 to your Clash roll (Phase 3).                                                 |
| Disrupted Formation | Enemy suffers −1 to both Charge and Clash rolls.                                    |
| Seized Initiative   | If you win the overall battle, the enemy takes \+1d2 extra injury in the aftermath. |

**Tie-breaker:** If tied, both sides reroll (up to 3 rerolls). If still tied after 3 rerolls, no benefit is granted and neither side scores a counter point for this phase.

## **Phase 2: The Charge (Morale)**

Both sides roll 1d20 \+ Morale. The shock of first contact. The Charge winner gains \+1 to their Clash roll and scores a counter point.

**Tie-breaker:** If tied, both sides reroll once. If still tied, neither side gains the Clash bonus.

## **Phase 3: The Clash (Vitality)**

Both sides roll 1d20 \+ Vitality. The grinding melee that decides the battle. The Clash winner scores two counter points.

**Tie-breaker:** If tied, the melee was brutal and indecisive, and no one gains counter points.

## **Determining the Victor**

After all three phases, tally the battle counter:

| Result                | Counter                                                    |
| :-------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| Win a phase           | \+1 (+2 for The Clash)                                     |
| Win with a natural 20 | \+1 bonus counter point on top of the phase’s normal value |
| Lose a phase          | −1                                                         |
| Lose with a natural 1 | −1 extra penalty on top of the phase’s normal loss         |

**Final tie-breaker:** If the counter totals are tied after all phases, a sudden-death contested Vitality roll breaks the tie. If that also ties, reroll until one side wins.

# **Aftermath**

After each battle, all legions that fought (both winner and loser) make the following four checks. Outcomes differ based on whether the legion won or lost.

## **Recovery Check (Vitality)**

**Roll:** 1d20 \+ Vitality vs DC (12 \+ current injuries).

| Outcome        | Winner    | Loser      |
| :------------- | :-------- | :--------- |
| Check succeeds | No injury | 1 injury   |
| Check fails    | 1 injury  | 2 injuries |

Natural 1 always fails. At 6 injuries, a legion is destroyed and removed from the battle (7 for legions with the Bulwark tag).

## **Hope Check (Morale)**

**Roll:** 1d20 \+ current Morale vs DC 12\. A legion’s Morale cannot exceed 10\. Gains and losses from Hope checks are subject to this cap.

| Outcome        | Winner     | Loser     |
| :------------- | :--------- | :-------- |
| Check succeeds | \+2 Morale | −1 Morale |
| Check fails    | \+1 Morale | −2 Morale |

**Rout:** If Morale drops to 0, the legion routs: it retreats up to 3 sections toward the nearest allied territory and cannot act next round (unless it has the Relentless tag). If its Morale rises above 0, it rallies and may act normally. If an enemy legion moves into a routed legion’s section while it has 0 Morale and no other allied legion is present, the routed legion is disbanded. A routed legion that survives the round uncontested benefits from Phase 5 recovery as normal (+1 Morale), which is enough to rally it.

## **Salvage Check (Wit)**

**Roll:** Both winner and loser roll 1d20 \+ Wit vs DC 12\. On success, choose one benefit. On a natural 20, choose two.

| Salvage Benefit   | Effect                                                                  |
| :---------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Captured Supplies | Remove 1 injury from this legion.                                       |
| Tactical Insight  | \+1d2 to this legion’s Wit rolls next round.                            |
| Enemy Shaken      | The opposing legion suffers −1 Morale.                                  |
| Quick Fortify     | Immediately fortify the section you hold (normally takes a full round). |

## **Commander Casualty Check**

After every battle, both commanders must make a survival check to endure the chaos. The battle’s outcome generates a Base Death Chance. The commander’s legion attempts to shield them, reducing this risk before a d100 is rolled.

| Situation                        | Base Death Chance |
| :------------------------------- | :---------------- |
| Won the battle                   | 6%                |
| Lost the battle                  | 12%               |
| Lost by 3+ on the battle counter | 20%               |

**To resolve the check:**

1. Determine the Base Death Chance from the table above.

2. Calculate Protection: the legion’s current Morale is the Protection value.

3. Subtract Protection from the Base Death Chance to find the Final Death Target. This target cannot fall below 1%.

4. Roll 1d100. If the result is equal to or lower than the Final Death Target, the commander falls in battle.

_Example: A legion with 5 Morale loses a battle. Base Death Chance is 12%. Subtract 5 Protection, resulting in a Final Death Target of 7%. On a 1–7, the commander dies. On an 8+, they survive._

_Example: A legion with 2 Morale loses by 3+ on the counter. Base Death Chance is 20%. Subtract 2 Protection, Final Death Target is 18%. This is extremely dangerous._

_Example: A legion with 8 Morale wins the battle. Base Death Chance is 6%. Subtract 8, capped to minimum 1%. Only a roll of 1 kills the commander._

**On commander death:** The legion immediately loses 1 Morale. A replacement commander from the faction’s reserve pool takes command at the start of the next round, bringing their own tags. If no reserves remain, the legion operates without a commander (no tags apply).

Commander death should be a dramatic moment. For allied commanders, give the players time to react. For enemy commanders, narrate the chaos in the opposing ranks as their leader falls.

# **Major Events**

At dramatic moments during the battle, the DM triggers a major event that pulls some or all PCs into a full D\&D encounter. If the players resolve a major event in the allied side’s favor, they earn a permanent bonus that shifts the balance of the wider battle.

**Icarus Subdued or Calmed** — The sight of a dragon fighting to protect the city rather than destroy it is a turning point in the hearts of Mytros’ defenders. **Reward:** \+2 to the allied Miracle pool.

**Acastus Redeemed** — When Acastus hands over the Rod of Rulership, his seven dragonlord captains lose their reason to fight. **Reward:** \+2 to the allied Miracle Points, Acastus joins the battle as a powerful Commander.

**The Colossus Awakened** — The sight of the city’s great guardian rising fills the defenders with renewed purpose while sowing dread in the enemy ranks. **Reward:** \+2 to the allied Miracle pool. The Colossus can move 2 sections per round. The section it occupies is considered fortified by allied forces.

**Hergeron Driven from the Temple of the Five** — Hergeron, son of Sydon, leads a deliberate assault on the Temple of the Five. **Reward:** \+2 to the allied Miracle pool.

**Sydon Defeated** — The Lord of Storms falls. The tremendous storm that bursts from Sydon’s defeat extinguishes all fires across the city. **Reward:** \+2 to the allied Miracle pool. Halve all ongoing deaths from destroyed objectives for the rest of the battle.

**Lutheria Defeated** — With the Titan of Death gone, her transformations unravel. **Reward:** \+2 to the allied Miracle pool. Subtract 800 from the running Death Toll.

**Kentimane Defeated** — The Hundred-Handed One falls, and the earth is still. **Reward:** \+2 to the allied Miracle pool. Death Toll accumulation stops entirely — the fighting is over. This is a battle-ending event.

# **Civilian Death Toll**

The battle for Mytros is not just a military contest — every round of fighting costs the city its people. Track a running Death Toll alongside the battle map. At the end of each round’s Aftermath phase, add that round’s deaths to the cumulative total. This total determines how the city remembers the heroes when the dust settles.

After each battle resolves, roll once per engagement and once per destroyed objective still burning:

| Situation                                      | Death Roll |
| :--------------------------------------------- | :--------- |
| Allied legion won the battle                   | 1d4 × 10   |
| Allied legion lost the battle                  | 1d6 × 50   |
| Sydon’s legion was not engaged this round      | 1d6 × 50   |
| Each destroyed Strategic Objective (per round) | 1d4 × 10   |

Add all results together to get the round’s death count, then add it to the running total.

# **Miracles**

Each side possesses a pool of Miracle Points per round. Typically, Sydon starts with 10 points, while the allied forces start with 8\.

## **How to Use Points**

These points are a flexible resource that can be spent to influence the tide of war:

-   **Roll Bonuses**: Distribute points as flat bonuses to any rolls made during the round (Battle rolls, Aftermath rolls, or Reconnaissance).
-   **Divine Advantage (2 Points)**: Grant advantage on any roll before its made.
-   **Divine Restoration**: A PC can restore their strength.
    -   **Short Rest (2 Points)**: One PC immediately gains the benefits of a Short Rest.
    -   **Long Rest (4 Points)**: One PC immediately gains the benefits of a Long Rest.

Miracle pools can shift during the battle. If heroes achieve spectacular victories, the allied pool might increase. If a temple is destroyed, Sydon’s pool grows. Let the story drive the numbers.
